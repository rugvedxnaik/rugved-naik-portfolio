Collectibility Over Loyalty

A March 2026 consumer-research dossier on why fragrance customers were collecting, not simply converting.

Academic project, completed March 2026.

Role

Led quantitative review analysis within a mixed-method netnography, coding individual ratings and reviews alongside social listening.

Problem

Retention language risked confusing collectors with loyalists.

Evidence

4,700+ ratings and reviews, competitive benchmarking, attribute mining and social listening.

Decision

Separate object value from identity value before deciding product and GTM moves.

Artifact

Identity Value x Object Value framework.

Outcome

A decision framework separating collectibility from loyalty and translating the distinction into product and GTM choices.

Résumé FR

J'ai piloté l'analyse quantitative de plus de 4 700 avis pour distinguer valeur identitaire et valeur d'objet, puis traduire ce signal en logique de collection.

Evidence trail

The claim is separated into timing, research scale, work owned and research boundaries.

The signal

I do not stay loyal to a fragrance. I build a collection that says who I am becoming. Composite signal synthesized from recurring review and social-listening language, not a verbatim quote.

Method and limitations

Fragrance brands often lean on a comfortable story: find your signature scent, wear it forever, become known for it. That story is easy to build around because it turns repeat purchase into loyalty and loyalty into identity.

The early signal suggested something more specific. People seemed attached to the object, the display, and the ritual of choosing, but that did not prove the scent had become a durable part of the self. The only way to separate those forms of attachment was to read the behavior at scale.

Unit of analysis: individual ratings and reviews, read for attribute-level language rather than star ratings alone.

Coding lens: object signals such as bottle, packaging, display and collectibility versus identity signals such as confidence, signature and self-recognition.

Triangulation: review language, rating patterns, competitive benchmarks and Instagram and TikTok behavior.

Boundary: reviews overrepresent purchase, unboxing and early ownership. The framework is a launch read, not a longitudinal loyalty study.

The evidence showed recurring object and display language, while durable identity language was less developed. Because the source archive did not preserve a publishable platform-by-platform count or coded percentage table, this dossier treats the pattern as directional rather than representative of the full market.

The tension underneath the data

Object value and identity value are not opposites. They often coexist, which is why ordinary retention metrics flatten them into one thing: brand love. But the behaviors are different. A collector wants more objects. A loyalist wants the same one again.

If a collector base is managed with a loyalist playbook, the system rewards the wrong behavior. It pushes repeat purchase of one item when the stronger desire is range, display, completion, and the ability to keep adding to the set.

The translation: Identity Value x Object Value

The framework separates attachment to the product as a thing from attachment to the product as a statement about the self. Object Value covers the bottle, packaging, shelf appeal, collectibility, and social display. Identity Value covers confidence, attraction, signature ritual, and self-recognition.

Read through this framework, the language was directionally richer around Object Value than durable Identity Value. That made the strategic move clear: build for the collection behavior that was already present, then connect it to a sharper identity territory.

Research foundation

The framework builds on Russell Belk's work on possessions and the extended self and Arjun Appadurai's work on the social life and circulation of things. Its contribution here is practical: separating attachment to the collectible object from evidence that one fragrance has become a durable identity anchor.

Output

Product implication: make the collection itself the product, with distinct releases, visible object codes, shelf-worthy packaging, and a reason to keep expanding the set.

GTM implication: move beyond "find your signature scent" and frame the shelf, the range, and the act of curation as the reason to return.

Evidence boundary and transferability

The evidence supports a distinction between object value and identity value in this launch context. It does not establish population-level loyalty or causality. The framework is useful beyond fragrance only when the same signals can be observed and tested in the new category.

Later brand activity

Later Miu Miu releases echoed the object, range and ritual logic. They are shown as subsequent brand activity, not as proof of causality or influence.
