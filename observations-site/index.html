Point of view

Consumer centric is not soft. It is operational.

A product decision asks what someone is trying to get done, signal, avoid, or become. A marketing decision asks what they should remember after the touchpoint disappears.

The case matters, but the consumer signal comes first. The company appears only after the behavior has been read and translated into a usable system.

For a PM, that means fewer features guessed at and more features earned from evidence. For a PMM, that means positioning that is remembered instead of positioning that is merely accurate.

What I read

A lens before the signals.

01 / collection

Some people do not stay loyal to a fragrance. They build a collection that says who they are becoming.

02 / identity

Beauty is not only applied. It is assembled through planes, edges, colors, and permission.

03 / category noise

When every brand says protein, gut health, and better-for-you, stated preference becomes crowded evidence.

04 / memory

AI can personalize the journey, but brand meaning has to stay stable enough to be remembered.

05 / recognition

Prediction can be accurate and still feel cold. Recognition begins when the product remembers the person, not only the next action.

06 / infrastructure

Shared data can improve service, but the front-of-house behavior still has to feel specific to the house.

Traveling across India, I have watched how the same consumer behaviors, collecting, ritual, trust in claims, and loyalty, show up differently by region, income, and generation. That is the same lens applied outside a brand brief.
