Point of view

Consumer centric is not soft. It is operational.

A product decision asks what someone is trying to get done, signal, avoid, or become. A marketing decision asks what they should remember after the touchpoint disappears.

I work in that space between the person and the system: the behavior that explains the opportunity, the tension that makes the strategy necessary, and the structure that lets product, brand, and marketing move together.

Collectibility Over Loyalty

Why Miutine behaves more like a luxury object than a signature fragrance.

This is the evidence anchor of the portfolio. The work started with a concrete dataset and used consumer language to separate being admired, being collected, and being personally adopted.

Evidence base

4,700+ reviews, competitive benchmarks, attribute-level review mining, and Instagram plus TikTok social listening.

Behavior

Consumers praised the bottle and object codes even when longevity weakened attachment.

Tension

Visibility and satisfaction were present, but identity drivers like confidence, sex appeal, and compliments were weaker.

Framework

Identity Value x Object Value separated collectible appeal from signature-fragrance attachment.

Output

Review mining, competitive read, social listening, framework, and recommendations.

Status

Full evidence-backed case now in the portfolio.

Face Architecture

A beauty territory that translates couture construction into product, service, ritual, and seasonal expression.

The point was not simply to say couture can inspire makeup. The work asked what Givenchy could own in beauty that competitors could not borrow as easily: the construction of the face as a repeatable house logic.

Behavior

Beauty consumers use the face to construct identity through plane, line, volume, and accent.

Tension

Couture had authority, but beauty needed a repeatable meaning beyond fashion adjacency.

Move

Turn the face into architecture and make construction the organizing idea.

Output

Positioning, concept system, experience logic, and service cues.

Proof level

Finished concept dossier, presented as a sprint to the Givenchy beauty team.

What it shows

Consumer identity can become a product and brand system.

Claim Saturation

When every competitor learns the same nutrition insight, which signals are still worth researching?

This is deliberately marked as an inquiry, not a finished case. The open question is how a food brand can avoid chasing the same claims everyone else has already learned to make.

Category signal

Protein, probiotics, reformulation, and better-for-you benefits are converging across dairy and adjacent nutrition.

Research risk

Consumer work can start measuring category-wide noise instead of useful differentiation.

Lens

Look for where behavior contradicts stated preference: what people buy, repeat, trade off, or ignore.

Output

Framework outline for consumer insight, category reading, and claim-space prioritization.

Next proof

Category map, claim audit, shelf examples, and sharper evidence on where stated preference and buying behavior diverge.

What it shows

Category saturation can become a research question, not just a positioning problem.

Applied to target house Active inquiry Beauty AI and recognizable codes

What AI Can't Personalize

As AI personalizes discovery, beauty brands still need cues that stay recognizable.

This is an inquiry about restraint. If every message, recommendation, and journey adapts to the individual, the brand still needs fixed cues people can recognize without thinking too hard.

Question

What must remain consistent for a beauty brand to stay recognizable across personalized journeys?

Rule

The closer something is to memory, the less it should be personalized. The closer something is to utility, the more it can adapt.

Behavior

Consumers want recognition without being reduced to past clicks.

Output

A rule set for separating adaptive utility from stable brand cues.

Next proof

Beauty examples where personalization improves usefulness, plus examples where it weakens recognition.

What it shows

Personalization strategy needs memory cues, not just targeting accuracy.

Applied to target house Active inquiry Luxury group customer intelligence

Shared Infrastructure, House-Specific Expression

Can luxury groups share customer intelligence without making every house feel the same?

This stays separate from Givenchy. Givenchy is a house-level product and identity territory. LVMH is a group-level question about what can be shared behind the scenes while each house keeps its own behavior in front of the customer.

Group question

Shared data can improve CRM, service, and relevance across a luxury portfolio.

Luxury risk

The same efficiency can flatten house memory if every brand starts behaving alike.

Distinction

This is group-level infrastructure thinking, distinct from Givenchy's house-level product territory.

System

Shared intelligence, house-specific expression.

Next proof

House code map, service moments, and customer use cases that show where shared data should stop.

What it shows

Good data architecture has to protect meaning, not just increase efficiency.

19h03

A functional sparkling aperitif built around the precise moment the day becomes evening.

This is a finished concept dossier rather than a research-heavy case. It shows how one occasion, the first evening drink, can organize product benefit, social meaning, visual codes, and brand world.

Behavior

People use the first drink as a social and emotional transition.

Tension

Non-alcoholic and functional drinks often define themselves by what is missing.

Move

Own the moment of transition rather than the substitute category.

Output

Occasion insight, product concept, positioning, and brand atmosphere.

Proof level

Finished concept dossier showing how a behavior becomes product logic and brand world.

What it shows

Consumer ritual can become a product proposition.

Method

The work moves from signal to system.

  1. 01Observe

    Find the behavior, ritual, review pattern, search language, claim, or moment of choice.

  2. 02Separate

    Distinguish useful evidence from category noise, polished claims, and obvious preferences.

  3. 03Name

    Turn the pattern into language a team can remember and challenge.

  4. 04Translate

    Move the insight into product logic, positioning, messaging, CRM, or experience design.

  5. 05Make it travel

    Build the deck, dossier, framework, or phrase that lets the work keep moving.
