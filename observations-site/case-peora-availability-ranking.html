Amazon advertising / E-commerce performance / Executive dashboards

Making Availability Visible

How connecting Amazon advertising, inventory, sales and organic visibility changed the operating response.

Freelance client work. Completed commercial analysis and operating dashboard.

Role

Managed Amazon advertising and e-commerce performance for Peora's international marketplace account, alongside analytics and expansion work.

Problem

Ranking declines around stock-out periods were being treated primarily as SEO or advertising issues.

Evidence

Inventory availability, organic keyword ranking, sales and advertising data combined across marketplaces.

Decision

Treat availability as a commercial risk consistent with rank loss, not only as a fulfillment detail.

Artifact

Excel and Power BI executive dashboard linking stock-outs, rank loss, ad dependency and recovery risk.

Operational change

Created one recurring operating view across advertising, inventory, ranking and sales, replacing a content-only diagnosis with shared commercial monitoring.

Résumé FR

Mission freelance reliant stock, ventes, publicité et visibilité organique dans un dashboard de pilotage. Le signal observé est compatible avec un effet de la disponibilité sur le classement, sans prétendre isoler l'algorithme Amazon.

Operating hypothesis

If I can't buy it right now, I stop looking for it. Strategic paraphrase used to express the observed marketplace pattern, not a verbatim consumer quote.

Why this needed a real investigation, not a dashboard refresh

The business had a recurring problem: certain products lost organic search ranking around stock-out periods and did not immediately recover when inventory returned. Leadership's working theory was that this was a search-optimization issue: something about the listing, the keywords, or the content needed fixing.

That explanation was actionable but incomplete. Fixing a listing is a straightforward task with a clear owner, while the availability pattern was harder to see because the evidence sat across several operating systems. The data existed to check the theory, but it was scattered across five disconnected systems: Seller Central, Amazon Advertising, inventory reports, sales reports, and organic ranking tools.

  • Inventory availability, organic keyword rankings, sales performance, and advertising performance were integrated into one Excel and Power BI view.
  • The sequence around every stock-out event was tracked product by product, instead of reading ranking dips in isolation.
  • The integrated view became a recurring executive dashboard, so the pattern could be monitored going forward.

The combined view showed a sequence consistent with availability contributing to rank loss: stock-outs coincided with lower sales velocity, weaker organic visibility and greater reliance on paid placement. The analysis did not isolate Amazon's ranking algorithm or claim a single causal mechanism.

The tension underneath the data

The default response to a ranking drop was to spend more on advertising to compensate. That is rational in the short term because paid placement can mask an organic ranking problem. But it also increases dependency on paid media to fix a problem paid media did not cause and cannot permanently solve.

The observed pattern was consistent with the platform reading a consumer's inability to buy as a weaker relevance signal, although the analysis could not isolate Amazon's ranking mechanism. Operationally, the business was budgeting as if advertising were the default lever whenever performance dipped, while availability remained a plausible contributor to the loss of visibility.

The translation: availability as a ranking input, not a fulfillment detail

The framework reframes inventory planning as a marketing-adjacent function, not a purely logistical one. Stock availability is not only about whether a sale can happen today. In this analysis, it was a contributor consistent with the later loss of organic visibility, not a proven explanation of Amazon's algorithm.

Once that is understood, an inventory shortfall stops being an operations-team problem to quietly manage and becomes a commercial-performance risk that marketing, inventory, and leadership need to see at the same time.

Operating response

Commercial monitoring: connect inventory alerts to organic visibility, sales and advertising performance so approaching stock-outs are visible before paid media becomes the default response.

Advertising decision: separate spend used to generate demand from spend used to compensate for an availability-related visibility problem.

Measurement boundary

The integrated view made the account-level sequence visible, but the public archive does not preserve a publishable count of stock-out events, rank-loss magnitude or recovery lag. The dossier therefore reports the operating change without presenting the pattern as a new discovery about Amazon's algorithm.
