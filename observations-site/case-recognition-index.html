Product strategy / Data model / Personalization

Prediction is not the same as recognition.

The Recognition Index is a dashboard-ready workbook for comparing how products personalize: whether they only predict the next action, become relevant in context, recognize the person, or strengthen identity.

Evidence base

A 25-product sample dataset across beauty, consumer tech, food and beverage, and digital products.

Retainable line

Personalization feels warmer when the product remembers the person, not only the next action.

Tool

Power BI-ready workbook with data and dashboard prep sheets.

Stages

Prediction, relevance, recognition, and identity.

Use

Executive storytelling, product diagnosis, and personalization strategy.

Signal

A product can be accurate and still feel cold.

The missing layer is often memory, ownership, and identity continuity.
01

Problem

Most personalization metrics reward prediction before recognition.

Product teams can optimize recommendation accuracy, click likelihood, and next-best action without asking whether the experience makes someone feel known in a way they value. The framework separates technical usefulness from emotional continuity.

Prediction

The system anticipates behavior.

Recognition

The experience preserves preference, memory, ownership, and identity.

02

Model

The workbook turns a soft feeling into a reviewable product score.

Each product is scored across staged maturity fields. The aim is not to flatten taste into a number. It is to make the tradeoff visible enough for a team to discuss: are we only getting better at guessing, or are we helping the user feel remembered?

Prediction score

How well the product anticipates behavior or need.

Recognition score

How well it remembers the person in a way that feels useful and respectful.

Identity score

Whether personalization supports who the user is becoming, not only what they might buy.

Product implication

Score products by recognition mechanisms, not only predictive accuracy or relevance. The better question is what the system remembers and why that memory should feel welcome.

03

Move

The GTM language changes from targeting to being remembered.

The strongest personalization stories do not say, “we know what you will do next.” They say, “we understand what should carry forward.” That shift makes personalization less creepy, more useful, and easier to defend as a consumer benefit.

What this proves

A consumer-centric product framework can connect metrics, UX, positioning, and trust in one operating language.

“The best personalization feels like continuity, not surveillance.”
