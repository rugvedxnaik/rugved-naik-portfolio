Product research / Sustainable mobility / Adoption systems

Cleaner mobility has to fit the city people already live in.

The foldable electric mobility project started from a practical adoption problem: a cleaner vehicle is not enough if it cannot handle storage, last-mile movement, transit handoffs, and the constraints of dense urban life.

Evidence base

User research, prototyping, simulation, feasibility analysis, an Indian patent filing, and published research.

Retainable line

Design for adoption conditions, not just vehicle function.

Problem

Last-mile movement in crowded urban infrastructure

Format

Patent-linked product research

System

Storage, transit, usability, and commercialization

Signal

I will use cleaner mobility only if it fits the city I actually live in.

Research published in the International Journal of Vehicle Structures and Systems.
01

Observation

The barrier was not only range or sustainability. It was everyday fit.

Urban mobility decisions are shaped by small frictions: where the vehicle is stored, how it moves through public transport, whether it can be carried, whether it feels usable in crowded streets, and whether the user can imagine it becoming part of a daily route.

Why it mattered

A sustainable product does not become adopted because the category is morally right. It becomes adopted when the behavior is possible.

Vehicle problem

Build a lighter, cleaner, compact electric mobility concept.

Adoption problem

Make the product fit storage, transit, last-mile behavior, and urban routines.

02

Tension

Innovation can be technically interesting and behaviorally inconvenient.

The core tension was between engineering possibility and the user's lived environment. A foldable electric product had to prove its mechanical idea, but also its place in the wider city system.

Evidence

The work combined user research, prototyping, simulation, feasibility analysis, and commercialization thinking after graduation as an independent venture.

“The product had to become small enough for the city, not only smart enough for the category.”

03

System

The concept organized mobility around adoption conditions.

Instead of treating the foldable mechanism as a feature, the project treated it as a response to a pattern: people need cleaner last-mile movement that can disappear into the rest of the day when not in use.

Space

Compactness and storage as adoption infrastructure.

Movement

Public transport integration and last-mile continuity.

Proof

Patent filing, published research, prototyping, and feasibility analysis.

Output

A foldable electric mobility product concept designed around urban space, sustainability, usability, and commercialization potential.

04

What it proves

Adoption is a design problem before it is a messaging problem.

The work belongs in this portfolio because it starts from behavior. It asks how people actually move, what their environment allows, and what the product must become before the promise of sustainability can translate into use.

Portfolio read

The consumer lens is not limited to campaigns. It can shape mechanical product thinking, adoption strategy, and commercialization logic.
